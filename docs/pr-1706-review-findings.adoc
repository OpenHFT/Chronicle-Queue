== Summary

Do not merge the change in its current shape. The alias hazard that the PR
describes is real and the proof below the alias period is sound. The cost of
the fix is the problem. `SCQIndexing.lastSequenceNumber()` now rejects the
sequence tracker for every write position at or above `2^(64 - cycleShift)`
bytes, and falls back to a linear scan from the last index entry. On the sparse
roll cycles that scan runs on every write that follows a write by another
appender. Measured with JLBH on `HUGE_DAILY_XSPARSE`, a write costs 62 us at
10,000 records and 484 us at 100,000 records, where the base costs under 1 us.

== Why the fix is safe below the period

The appender publishes the write position (`StoreAppender.java:799` and
`:1362`) before the tracker (`:800` and `:1364`), under the write lock that
`closeCleanup` releases afterwards. The tracker is therefore at most one record
stale. A stable re-read of the write position below the period pins the pair
to that position exactly. Above the period the pair can only alias when the
previous record is exactly `k * period` bytes long.

=== JLBH benchmark

`src/test/java/net/openhft/chronicle/queue/bench/LastSequenceNumberJLBHBenchmark.java`

----
mvn -q exec:java -Dexec.classpathScope=test \
  -Dexec.mainClass=net.openhft.chronicle.queue.bench.LastSequenceNumberJLBHBenchmark \
  -DrollCycle=HUGE_DAILY_XSPARSE -Drecords=100000
----

Two appenders alternate writes on the harness thread, so every write finds the
position moved and `writeHeader` calls `lastSequenceNumber()`. The end-to-end
sample is one write. The probe samples `excerptsInCycle`. The queue starts with
a filler record that spans the alias period, then `records` small records.
Coordinated omission is off, so the numbers are service times. Median of runs
2 and 3, 2,000 iterations per run at 200/s, on one developer machine with the
default JVM flags. Use the ratios, not the absolute values.

[cols="2,1,1,1,1"]
|===
| Roll cycle, records | Write, base | Write, head | excerptsInCycle, base | excerptsInCycle, head

| HUGE_DAILY_XSPARSE, 10,000 | 0.67 us | 62 to 70 us | 17 to 23 us | 86 to 93 us
| HUGE_DAILY_XSPARSE, 100,000 | 0.7 to 1.5 us | 484 to 491 us | 22 to 26 us | 444 to 447 us
| HUGE_DAILY, 10,000 | 0.79 us | 3.2 to 3.3 us | 17 to 23 us | 23 to 28 us
|===

The head cost grows about 5 ns per record since the last index entry, hot in
cache. `HUGE_DAILY_XSPARSE` indexes every 1,048,576th record, so a write
reaches about 5 ms before the next index entry.

== Recommendation

Keep the tracker above the period and prove the pair another way. Two options
are exact and O(1), given that the tracker is at most one record stale:

. Skip the bound when a single record cannot span the period. A record length
  has at most 30 bits, so for `cycleShift <= 33` the alias cannot occur. This
  restores the standard cycles, `LARGE_HOURLY` and `SMALL_DAILY`, but not the
  large and sparse cycles.
. Above the period, read the header at `lastWritePos - period`. If that offset
  is below the first data position, or the record there does not end exactly
  at `lastWritePos`, the tracker cannot be one record stale by one period. A
  false "unproven" costs only the fallback, never a wrong answer.

Apply the same proof to the three sites that the PR leaves unchanged, or state
the scope in the PR body. Reconcile with PR #1705, which implements the same
change and is still open.
